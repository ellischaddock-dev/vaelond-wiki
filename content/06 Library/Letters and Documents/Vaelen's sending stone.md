My end is near. I am certain, even if I cannot yet name the hand that will finally reach me. If I am fortunate, this record will outlive me. If I am not, then perhaps it will merely serve as proof that at least one of us understood the charge laid upon us.

We were not meant to rule. That is the truth the others have forgotten. We were charged to guide the world, to prepare it, to leave behind something strong enough to resist should the ritual ever fail and Ardynia rise again. We were to cultivate endurance, not dominion. Readiness, not empire. We were never meant to lead the world by the throat.

Of them all, Erith has held closest to that truth. He built what he wished to build, gave Minir its structure, and then stepped away. Some would call that selfishness. Perhaps it is. He has spent his long years pursuing pleasure and his own amusements while the rest of us tangled ourselves in duty and fear. Yet there is honesty in him. He understood that this world was meant to stand on its own feet, not remain beneath ours. I do not admire his indifference, but I understand it better than the ambitions of the others.

Marra understands none of this. She believes our charge unfinished. Worse, she believes herself chosen to complete it. She wants the relics not to preserve them, but to wield them — to finish what Elgria began and destroy the threat of Ardynia utterly, whatever the cost. She speaks of necessity, as zealots always do when they wish to make horror sound disciplined. To her, my refusal to join her is proof that I stand against her. She cannot imagine restraint except as betrayal. I believe she comes for me, and I believe she will call it righteousness when she does.

Desmon is no better, only more vain. He has grown tired of immortality in mortal shape, tired of being served by the fettered masses of Perfid. The long years have not humbled him; they have only made him greedy for a grander form of survival. He looks upon Elgria’s ascension and sees not accident, not tragedy, but possibility. He wants to rise as she did — no, more than she did, because he would choose it. He has begun to think like a man who sees divinity as an attainment. I do not trust what he would become if given the means.

Perhaps Erith is the wiser of us. Willingly giving up power was the only honest act any of us committed after Elgria ascended. Yet even now I cannot follow him fully. He can watch the pieces fall. I still feel compelled to place my hand between the board and those who would overturn it. For that, I think I shall die.

I confess I do not meet that prospect of death with grace. I fear it. I fear ending. So I have made preparations. If my enemies take my body, they shall not so easily claim all of me. I have preserved what I can preserve. My soul, at least, will not be surrendered willingly to oblivion. If this is cowardice, then it is cowardice I embrace gladly.

— Vaelen
