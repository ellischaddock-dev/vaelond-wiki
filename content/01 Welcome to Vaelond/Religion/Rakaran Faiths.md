# Rakaran Faiths

In [[The Powers That Be#Rakara|Rakara]], where the clans each hold close ties to different gods, the variety of Vaelondian worship is especially clear. A [[Clan Temir|Temir]] fisherman might honour the Sea-Mother daily, yet still whisper a prayer to Ezaa before a long journey. An [[Clan Orvak|Orvak]] herder gives thanks to Orun for fertile fields but lights a candle to Rhozka when burying kin. The gods are many, and all are real, so the wise show respect to all.

Each clan tends to favour certain gods—[[Clan Temir|Temir]] with the Sea-Mother and Moon-Father, [[Clan Khaali|Khaali]] with Ashka and Virei, [[Clan Erzhin|Erzhin]] with Ezaa and Khurmog, and so on—but even within clans, individuals may feel a personal pull towards other deities. A warrior might wear a token of Ulvarn for strength in battle, while a young performer might paint symbols of Virei before a festival dance.

People offer thanks when crops grow well or storms pass safely, and they make requests when danger looms or luck runs dry. Even gods feared for their wrath, like Ashka or Talik, receive their due—to ignore them is to invite misfortune. Worship might be as simple as a whispered phrase at dawn, a coin left at a roadside shrine, or a formal visit to a local temple or spirit-tent.

For most in Rakara, the gods are not distant figures but present forces in the world: capricious, powerful and worth honouring. Faith is not blind devotion, but a conversation between mortals and the divine—offerings for blessings, prayers for safety and praise for favour received.

## Sea-Mother and Moon-Father

**Associated with:** Water, Moon, Tides, Cycles, Rebirth  
**Symbol:** A silver wave curled around a crescent moon

- **Sea-Mother** is nurturing but vast and unknowable, worshipped for her bounty and feared for her wrath.
- **Moon-Father** is distant and wise, often interpreted as the one who measures the Sea-Mother's moods with his rhythms.
- **Clerical schism:** Some sects say Moon-Father tempers the Sea-Mother's power; others say he tries to steal it.

### Myth

Long ago, the Moon-Father wandered the sky alone, shedding cold tears into the sea below. One night, his sorrow stirred the depths, and the Sea-Mother rose, her laughter drowning his sadness. They danced—ebb and pull—until their love birthed the tides. But when they quarrel, the sea storms rage and the moon weeps in eclipse.

### Prayer

> *“Moon-Father, calm her breath. Sea-Mother, cradle our sails. Let your rhythm carry us through storm and still.”*

## Orun, the Verdant Kin

**Associated with:** Nature, Growth, Earth, Fertility  
**Symbol:** A twisted horn filled with vines and grain

- Revered as the sustainer of herds, crops and kinship.
- Believed to dwell in sacred groves and earthen mounds.
- Worship often involves seasonal rites and fertility dances.

### Myth

When the first beast was hungry, Orun buried her heart beneath the plains. From it grew the first grain. Her blood turned into rivers, her breath into pollen. It is said she returns each spring to dance among the sprouts, barefoot and smiling.

### Prayer

> *“Orun of root and horn, bless our herds and seed. Let green life rise where your name is sung.”*

## Ashka the Ember-Dancer

**Associated with:** Fire, Chaos, Inspiration, Renewal  
**Symbol:** A spiral flame with dancing feet

- Both destroyer and muse; fire is feared and revered.
- Shamans say she speaks through dreams and madness.
- Some Khaali clans say she was born of lightning striking bone.

### Myth

Ashka was born when lightning struck the skull of a slain tyrant. She sprang from the bones laughing, clad in smoke. Her steps set the hills ablaze, but from their ashes rose bright wildflowers. She is chaos, and she is beauty.

### Prayer

> *“Ashka, whose laughter cracks the sky, strike me with your flame. Let my spirit burn wild and bright.”*

## Virei the Shattered Mask

**Associated with:** Art, Expression, Emotion, Madness  
**Favoured by:** Khaali, Voruun  
**Symbol:** A half-mask painted with abstract symbols

- Patron of performers, mad prophets and radical visionaries.
- Worshipped through ecstatic ritual, theatre and music.
- Seen as either a divine artist or a cosmic prankster.

### Myth

Virei sculpted the first faces from clay, then shattered their mould. From the shards came poetry, song and dream. She hides her face so that no one may copy her, and whispers inspiration through the cracks in the mind.

### Prayer

> *“Virei, mask-maker, soul-breaker, speak through my hands and voice. Let me create what even the gods fear to imagine.”*

## Ulvarn the Towering Sun

**Associated with:** Ambition, Power, Order, War  
**Symbol:** A blazing tower crowned with a crown of light

- Said to have forged the first empires in the age before clans.
- Worshipped by chieftains and those who seek dominion.
- Believed to test mortals with obstacles to strengthen the worthy.

### Myth

Ulvarn rose from the mountain's shadow, bearing a crown he forged from the sun itself. He cast down the false kings and raised his banner across the plains. It is said each victory adds another flame to his tower.

### Prayer

> *“Ulvarn of light and law, raise my path. Let none stand between me and the summit I seek.”*

## Ezaa of the Whispering Gale

**Associated with:** Wind, Influence, Speech, Travel  
**Symbol:** A bird's feather wrapped in a spiral gust

- Messenger god, patron of scouts, messengers and spies.
- Worshipped through wind-chimes and sky offerings.
- Erzhini traders pray to Ezaa before bartering or diplomacy.

### Myth

Ezaa was born from the breath of the world itself. With every word spoken, she grows. Her wings beat on every caravan's sail and every liar's tongue. The wind may be still, but she is always listening.

### Prayer

> *“Ezaa, whispering wind, guide my steps and sharpen my tongue. May my words ride your currents.”*

## Khurmog the Silent Deep

**Associated with:** Stone, Memory, Permanence, Ancestors  
**Symbol:** A square spiral engraved into stone

- Guardian of ancestral knowledge, cairns and tombs.
- Worshipped through cairn-building and stone-etching.
- Erzhini lorekeepers say Khurmog remembers what the wind forgets.

### Myth

Before time flowed, Khurmog sat beneath the mountains, etching memory into stone. He taught the ancestors to carve their stories, so even in death they would endure. It is said earthquakes are his slow, thoughtful turns.

### Prayer

> *“Khurmog, old and still, hold our names in stone. Let the weight of our deeds outlast the winds.”*

## Talik the Laughing Coin

**Associated with:** Trade, Luck, Trickery  
**Symbol:** A spinning coin showing a laughing face on one side and a crying face on the other

- Popular among merchants, gamblers and wandering folk.
- Stories claim Talik once tricked a god out of their domain.
- Seen as both a patron of freedom and a necessary chaos.

### Myth

Talik tricked a god of law into a wager, then won his own divinity in the deal. Ever since, he walks among markets and battlefields, flipping his coin. He offers luck—fair or foul—to those who gamble with fate.

### Prayer

> *“Talik, spinner of fortunes, let the coin land light in my favour. And if not—make the fall worth the tale.”*

## Rhozka the Veiled One

**Associated with:** Secrets, Death, Dreams, Passage  
**Symbol:** A black veil over a sleeping face

- Keeper of the final passage and hidden knowledge.
- Worship varies: some fear Rhozka as the ender, while others revere Rhozka as a gentle guide.
- Dream-diviners and death-singers invoke Rhozka in moments of loss.

### Myth

Rhozka was the first to pass beyond the veil, and the only one to return. They carry dreams in one hand and endings in the other. It is said that when they sing, the dead remember.

### Prayer

> *“Rhozka, veiled and knowing, walk with us in silence. When our breath stills, open the next door gently.”*
