# Session 15: Stewards, Saddles and Shambling Roots

> [!abstract] Session summary
> The new bastion gains staff and horses before the road to Rana Viam begins with a deadly shambling mound.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 15 |
| **Recorded** | Friday, 27 March 2026 at 18:34 |
| **Arc** | The road to Rana Viam |

With embassies burned, diplomats dead, and [[Somnolent]] now far too small for the scale of the troubles gathering around it, the Defracturers turned, at least briefly, to something resembling administration.

It was time to hire staff.

## A Household in Need of Order

The interviews for a new head steward were, in their own way, as dangerous as any dungeon.

First came Mariasz Salhum, a former chief steward from a Temiri trading vessel. She carried herself with the calm authority of someone who had long ago learned how to keep difficult people fed, organized, and alive. Strong-willed, sensible, and plainly competent, she immediately stood apart from the field.

Then came Gorbels Valebrew, a so-called coffee mage whose references were dubious, his manner erratic, and whose deep personal vendetta against Brewed Awakening suggested a man with as many grievances as recipes.

Igon Duft followed, a gaunt and deeply unsettling figure with a glass eye and the air of a man who could absolutely solve problems, though perhaps not in ways anyone would later be comfortable learning about.

Then there was Ghorgadush, a towering half-orc swaddled in a patchwork vest made from bits of assorted wildlife, speaking in the third person and presenting the group with freshly baked cookies as though this were the most natural thing in the world.

Finally came Derrick Lowgins, Terrick’s brother, whose interview somehow revealed less about his own capabilities than it did about the deeply worrying shape of Terrick’s private life. His hints at abuse and misery left an unpleasant stillness in the room.

In the end, the choice was no choice at all. Mariasz Salhum was appointed Head Steward, the only candidate who seemed remotely suited to the role. The others, fortunately or unfortunately, were still welcomed into lesser positions.

With the household somewhat settled, the Defracturers gathered and did something even rarer than hiring responsibly:

They agreed to put aside their personal vendettas.

At least for now.

## The Luncheon of Great Importance

The group knew they needed to project competence. They were no longer merely adventurers blundering from crisis to crisis. They were patrons, agents, and increasingly visible players in the politics of Somnolent. The next gathering had to go well.

Plans were made. Appearances mattered. Gorbels and Igon were to remain hidden away in the kitchen, out of sight and out of mind. That, naturally, did not happen. The new household steward either ignored or overruled that instruction, and the result was a spread of distinctly underwhelming pastries that did not quite match the grandeur the occasion demanded.

Still, the guests came.

Romayne Drayton, Alaaric Hammerfall, and Elmwood Bluevine all attended. Alaaric, in particular, arrived with the energy of a man on the brink of divine revelation, setting up what could only be described as a Pepe Silvia board of threads, clues, and frantic connective logic. Together, the group sifted through everything they had uncovered and finally began to shape the larger picture.

Several conclusions emerged.

Eden Carrion was almost certainly the loyal Crow they had been circling around for so long.

The Old Grey Pike, while clearly a dangerous figure with a bloodstained history, did not appear to have been directly involved in the immediate events. The House Bromere favour coin used to pay Buggie’s gang had once belonged to him, yes — but he had lost it to Eden in the gambling den.

The possibility that Anthea Bloodclot had in truth been Saint Marra no longer seemed absurd.

Most importantly, the group recognized that too many factions were converging on the same objective. Anthea’s circle, the Broken Fang, and others still unseen were all interested in the [[The Sundering of Ardynia|Ardynian]] ruins. If the Defracturers wanted answers, they would need to pursue that trail above all others.

The relics tied to the Sundering — names like the Staff of Broken Time and the Bands of Distortion — remained unaccounted for, and dangerously coveted.

The Broken Fang would need further watching, with Alaaric and Job agreeing to keep a close eye on them.

Then Elmwood took up the scroll the party had recovered from Tace Nox and offered the next step: another Ardynian site, Rana Viam. He could not say precisely what waited there, but he knew enough to warn them that it would not be a quick excursion. Whatever lay within, they should prepare for a long stay.

He also kindly offered to cure Ballar’s petrified goat, only for Ballar to discover, to his embarrassment, that he could not actually produce it from the Bag of Rust Tricks at all.

## Provisions and Horses

Romayne, for his part, proved generous. He supplied the group with adventuring packs, ample rations, and pushed along Erelmor’s studded leather order so it would be ready in time. As one final gesture, he sent them to Marda to secure horses for the road ahead.

Marda loved her animals with the intensity of a devout priestess, and she made it immediately clear that she would not tolerate fools riding them. This seemed aimed quite specifically at Ballar, who became instantly obsessed with Kingcup, the most vain and unmanageable beast in the stable.

Marda had recommendations, and to their credit, most of the group listened.

Enrak was paired with Bracken, a slow but incredibly steady mount, the kind that would remain upright even if its rider did not.

Ieuan took Cobb, another dependable horse whose calm and sturdiness made it a natural extension of his own temperament.

n’Dong was given Nail, a former courier horse: quick, enduring, and somewhat shy when battle loomed.

Erelmor received Vesper, an elegant dark horse suited equally to grace and stealth.

And Ballar, ignoring all good sense, chose Kingcup — beautiful, dramatic, difficult, and clearly bred for parade grounds rather than warfare.

It was, in other words, a perfect match.

## The Road to Rana Viam

With provisions packed and horses at least partially under control, the Defracturers finally set out for Rana Viam.

For the first few hours, the road was mercifully uneventful. Then they saw it: drag marks leading from the road into the brush. Something had been taken, or had taken something else.

Naturally, Erelmor went first to investigate.

Naturally, it went badly.

The moment he approached, a trap snapped shut. Vines lashed around him, dragging him violently into the bushes and knocking him unconscious almost at once.

Ballar reacted first, hacking at the grasping growths and trying to cut Erelmor loose before whatever had laid the trap could strike again.

Then it emerged.

A Shambling Mound, massive and sodden, rose from the greenery like the road itself had decided to kill them.

The group attacked hard and fast, first scrambling to get their horses to safety, then turning all their attention to the creature. Their confidence faded quickly as blow after blow seemed to do almost nothing. The beast absorbed punishment with horrifying ease, and for a few moments it seemed as though they might be carved apart beside the roadside.

But numbers, persistence, and sheer bloody-mindedness began to tell. Slowly, painfully, the Defracturers brought the thing down.

When at last it collapsed into a heap of rotting vegetation and swamp-flesh, the party stood catching their breath, shaken but victorious.

Then n’Dong, seeing not horror but opportunity, crouched by the corpse and harvested ten pieces of shambling mound jerky.

Because of course he did
