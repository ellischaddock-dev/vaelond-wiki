# Session 11: Ashes, Brands and Windfalls

> [!abstract] Session summary
> A Broken Fang lead, a failed safe robbery and Romayne’s formal patronage transform the party into agents of Somnolent.

## Shadows in the Woods

While fortunes were rising inside the One Ball Inn, something far uglier was unfolding in the dark beyond its walls. Determined not to let another Broken Fang lead slip through his claws, n’Dong pursued the two agents who had been sent to investigate the sabotaged whiskey drop. Erelmor followed close behind, quieter than usual, but just as intent. For once, they showed restraint. They shadowed the pair through the woods, waiting for a name, a location, a careless mention of a superior.

They got no name. What they did get was flame.

From their hidden vantage, they watched as the agents found n’Dong’s crude woodland shelter — and without hesitation, set it ablaze. The fire spread quickly. The makeshift home collapsed inward in a shower of sparks and smoke. The restraint ended there.

The two Defracturers burst from the shadows like hunting beasts. Steel and spellwork ended the fight swiftly — a short, brutal skirmish beneath crackling firelight. But killing them wasn’t enough. They needed information.

The leader of the pair, a man known as **Sunshine**, broke first. Between blows and blood, he offered a scrap of value: The Broken Fang’s operations in [[Rakara]] were overseen by **Rhul Slowdrift**.

It was the last word he would ever speak. Sunshine’s throat was cut. His companion drowned in a barrel of stolen whiskey — forgotten, a grim ending. Then, in a final act of caution — or maybe cruelty — the bodies were burned. An “accident.” Just two careless smugglers and a poorly tended fire. By the time the flames died down, n’Dong had his name, but no home.

## Double or Nothing

Inside the warmth and lamplight of the One Ball Inn, the tension was no less sharp. When The Old Grey Pike leaned forward and growled “Double or nothing?” Ballar didn’t hesitate, accepting the challenge with gusto.

What Ballar had envisioned as a private duel of nerve quickly spiralled when Billy Hill and Summer Quaresh inserted themselves into the wager. Pride and greed outweighed etiquette as the stakes swelled. Before the dice rolled, Alaaric Hammerfall quietly pulled Ieuan and Enrak aside. His message was calm, but pointed: “Your lizard friend is making dangerous enemies. Antoinne isn’t forgiving. Keep an eye on him.”

The game began, a sudden-death format with high tension and high stakes. The Pike played cold and clinical — but fate turned on him first. He was eliminated early, but not quietly. Chairs scraped. Voices rose. He made sure the entire room heard him threaten Ballar — promising to see him at the dueling pits, his flare lingering long after he exited.

Ballar immediately attempted to negotiate — offering to split the pot three ways so he could leave and tail the Pike, but Billy and Summer refused, continuing the game. Their greed would be their downfall as the final roll settled, leaving Ballar victorious and 1,200 gold richer.

1,200 gold.

## The Silent Court

While fortunes shifted and tempers flared at the One Ball Inn, n’Dong and Erelmor pressed forward with the second half of the evening’s plan: infiltrating the Silent Court and uncovering whatever secrets lay within Alaaric Hammerfall’s safe.

The building loomed over them in the dim light, austere and unwelcoming even at rest. After circling the perimeter, they discovered what appeared to be a discreetly concealed entrance — not something meant for public use, but clearly known to someone with reason to avoid the front door. With quiet determination, they pried it open. Unfortunately, in a lapse of judgment that would define the rest of their evening, they left the hidden door propped open with a crowbar rather than securing their exit properly.

Inside, confidence began to erode quickly. The Silent Court was larger and more labyrinthine than anticipated, and neither of them actually knew where Alaaric’s safe was located. What began as cautious exploration soon devolved into aimless searching, opening office doors without method, scanning rooms with rising impatience, and trying to appear purposeful while having no real plan. The longer they remained inside, the more fragile their composure became.

That fragility shattered when they heard the metallic scrape behind them. The crowbar fell. The hidden door slammed shut.

The sound carried through the corridor like a verdict.

Panic surged through them both, but n’Dong acted immediately. Without hesitation, he shifted his form, adopting the appearance of Alaaric Hammerfall himself. The transformation was convincing — not merely facial, but posture, expression, even the subtle irritation of a bureaucrat inconvenienced by lesser minds. Erelmor, recognizing the risk, vanished into shadow and let the disguise carry them forward.

When guards approached to investigate the disturbance, n’Dong held his nerve. With curt authority and the air of a man annoyed at being questioned, he redirected suspicion and dismissed them. The deception held. The guards withdrew. It was an audacious recovery — and it bought them just enough time to press on.

Eventually, after more tense wandering, they located the safe. It was not a simple lockbox hidden behind a painting. It was a deliberate construct of layered defenses: heavy mechanical locks reinforced with runes etched into the metal casing, abjuration magic humming beneath the surface, and something older and less easily categorized woven into its framework. Even without fully understanding it, they could feel the complexity radiating from it.

They studied it carefully at first. They traced the runes, examined the locks, whispered theories about how to bypass the enchantments. The conclusion was clear long before either of them voiced it — they were not equipped for this. And yet, neither of them was willing to walk away empty-handed.

When they attempted to force the mechanism, the safe responded immediately. There was no dramatic explosion, no thunderous alarm. Instead, a focused surge of psychic energy erupted outward in a silent, devastating pulse. The sensation was intimate and invasive — not merely pain, but violation. Both n’Dong and Erelmor felt something burn into their flesh, a searing heat that carved sigils into their arms as if pressed there by invisible branding irons.

The safe did not open. It marked them. The weight of their mistake settled in quickly. This was not a trap meant to kill. It was meant to identify.

Realizing how exposed they now were, the pair abandoned any further attempt and fled. n’Dong dropped the illusion of Alaaric, reverting to his true form, but immediately concealed himself behind the visage of Virei’s Shattered Mask, understanding that his distinctive appearance would be remembered.

## Marks, Mandates & War

By the time the full party regrouped at the Drowsy Coin, the night had grown heavy with consequence. Callum Redmayne met them at the door, unusually serious, and informed them that Romayne Drayton required their presence at Town Hall at 9:00am sharp. There was no room for negotiation in his tone. Whatever was coming next would not wait for hangovers.

Once seated and secured, the recounting began.

Ballar, Ieuan, and Enrak listened in stunned silence as n’Dong and Erelmor revealed the chaos of their evening — the Broken Fang agents, the name *Rhul Slowdrift*, the burning of bodies to disguise murder, the infiltration of the Silent Court, and worst of all, the magical brands seared into their arms when they tampered with Alaaric’s safe. The room grew quieter with each revelation.

They examined the marks carefully. n’Dong sketched the runes as best he could remember them, crude lines drawn with shaking hands. They compared theories, searched for meaning, tried to determine whether the brand was tracking magic, a curse, or something worse.

They made no progress.

It was Ballar, of all people, who broke the spiral of speculation. “Let’s go now. Confess everything to Drayton.” It was not a comfortable suggestion — but it was the only clear one.

And so, long after decent folk had gone to bed, the Defracturers marched to Town Hall and demanded that Romayne be woken immediately. His attendant resisted at first, but the collective urgency of the group wore him down.

Romayne emerged groggy and visibly strained, already carrying the weight of the coming day. He listened with thin patience as they explained their break-in and the branding.

Through gritted teeth, he informed them that whatever they had done tonight paled in comparison to what awaited [[Somnolent]] at sunrise.

He assured them they were in no immediate danger from the marks on Alaaric’s safe — and that if the safe had truly wanted them dead, it would have done so. He implored them to rest and return at the appointed hour. All would be explained in the morning.

Reluctantly, they obeyed.

## Counsel at Dawn

After finally taking a long-overdue rest, Ballar rose early and went to see Ouin Brond. She was not amused.

Her usual sharp sarcasm had softened into something more measured. When Ballar attempted to recount his successes, she brushed past the bravado and spoke plainly instead. Glory, she explained, is often mistaken for purpose. Nations and families demand loyalty, but they rarely offer clarity in return. If he wanted to be more than a weapon in someone else’s war, he would need to choose his own path.

And when Ballar scoffed at her wisdom, she calmly demonstrated her foresight by having Brewed Awakening deliver a perfectly made cortado — exactly as he liked it. It was both humbling and irritating.

## The Announcement

The Defracturers arrived at Town Hall slightly late, to find it already crowded. Inside stood: Alaaric Hammerfall, Deputy Gayle Bramble, Ouin Brond and Petra Murwood, Ambassador of [[The Powers That Be#Minir|Minir]].

Romayne looked older than he had the night before. With a heavy breath, he delivered his decision. [[The Powers That Be#Perfid|Perfid]]’s diplomatic immunity was officially revoked in Somnolent. The justification was clear: their involvement in the murder of Anthea Bloodclot and the abduction of Terrick Lowgins.

The room shifted. This was no longer intrigue. This was escalation. Romayne made it plain: Perfid would respond. And while his limited forces would focus on holding Prusset, he would need the Defracturers to do something far more dangerous. They were to storm the Perfid Embassy and rescue Terrick.

Petra Murwood excused herself almost immediately, offering polite wishes of success while preserving Minir’s plausible deniability. Ouin, however, remained.

She recognized the opportunity for [[The Powers That Be#Doro|Doro]], but also the peril for the Defracturers. She challenged Romayne directly: if he intended to send them into open conflict, he needed to formally back them. It was a situation Romayne was well prepared for as Alaaric produced pre-drafted contracts.

Romayne offered them patronage under Somnolent, granting them protection, resources, and diplomatic immunity for actions taken in the town’s defense. Ballar, however, would have to relinquish his formal status as Doro’s ambassador.

The incentives grew more personal as the discussion continued:

- Enrak was promised the group could use the Perfid Embassy as a base if they succeeded.
- Erelmor and n’Dong were quietly granted pardons for crimes committed in the last twenty-four hours — a concession delivered with just enough mischief to acknowledge what had happened.

The weight of the decision settled over them. This was no longer freelance adventuring. One by one, they signed as The Defracturers entered into formal patronage under Somnolent.

And with that, the next morning’s objective became clear: Storm the Perfid Embassy. Rescue Terrick. Prepare for the consequences.
