# Session 10: Saints, Spies and Snake Eyes

> [!abstract] Session summary
> Prusset names the Church behind Anthea’s murder while the party infiltrates the One Ball Inn gambling circle.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 10 |
| **Recorded** | 04 January 2026 at 22:05 |
| **Arc** | Saints and spies |

With the ambassador in chains and the town still spinning from the chaos of the chase, Ieuan wasted no time. He found Romayne Drayton at the town hall and relayed the news: Prusset had been apprehended. The village headman didn’t hesitate. He swept scrolls from drawers into his bag and marched toward The Shackles with Ieuan at his side.

At the cells, Romayne verified everything with the stunned Deputy Bramble, then unleashed the magic of a Zone of Truth. While only Ieuan and Prusset fell under its sway, it was more than enough. The interrogation began, and with each question, the noose tightened.

Prusset, defeated and cornered, spilled everything:

- The [[Perfidian Faith|Church of Elgira]], acting through its High Illuminant, had ordered Anthea Bloodclot’s assassination.
- The Illuminant believed Anthea was actually Saint Marra, one of Elgira’s last living followers.
- This belief came not from faith, but from divine communion—the High Illuminant claimed to speak directly to Elgira.
- Mara (Anthea) was suspected of plotting to restore the [[The Sundering of Ardynia|Ardynian]] Empire.
- The Church feared her influence, and wanted her silenced.
- Worst of all, the [[Rootbound]] were used to enact the murder… though Prusset couldn’t name the specific agent.

The room chilled. None of them had expected to unearth a holy war.

In an unexpected moment of compassion, Ieuan asked about Terrick. Prusset confessed: the ranger had been abducted and taken to the [[The Powers That Be#Perfid|Perfid]] embassy. Romayne immediately dispatched Gayle Bramble to investigate, fury laced through his usually measured expression.

When asked why he had begged for his life so pathetically, Prusset was blunt — if Elgira’s church was willing to kill Anthea in [[Rakara]], what would they do to him after this betrayal? He didn’t fear the adventurers. He feared his own masters.

Romayne, ever the tactician, left him to rot in the cell, and returned to his office to ponder the growing threat of Perfid.

## Riverside Regrets & Rude Messages

Meanwhile, Ballar stood alone by the river, trying to find some shred of calm. He prayed—not to any one god, but to all those he thought might be listening. His meditation was shattered by a message spell from Ouin, to which he responded with deadpan sarcasm:

“Ballar isn’t here right now. Please leave a message and I will respond as soon as I can.”

Having vented, he returned to his room at the Drowsy Coin, where he finally spoke at length with the soul in his Ring of Mind Shielding—a conversation more civil than one might expect from the usually guarded paladin.

Not long after, the rest of the Defracturers arrived. Ieuan led with peace, admitting that he had given everything to Drayton, and had made no copies. Ballar’s fury flared but, perhaps softened by solitude and exhaustion, he chose understanding over conflict—for now.

Their thoughts turned to Ersil the Bard, whose songs had been painting targets on their backs for days. Ballar and Ieuan railed against the exposure, but Enrak, utterly baffled by their paranoia, declared it a boon. And slowly, the others came around. With so many enemies gathering, perhaps a little fame was a shield of its own.

## The Dice Game and the Pike

With the weight of politics briefly lifted, the group turned to their next objective: the gambling den at the One Ball Inn. Erelmor had a vendetta. The Old Grey Pike, he believed, was tied to the destruction of his home and the death of his wife.

Ballar needed information. He suspected the Pike once carried the House Bromere coin that funded Buggie’s mercenaries to kidnap Prusset. n’Dong wanted proof. He believed the Pike and the game were linked to Prodosius and the Broken Fang.

An uneasy pact was struck. Ballar promised Erelmor that, once the Pike had given up what he knew, the rogue could kill him. Satisfied—for now—Erelmor agreed to hold his blade.

## Infiltration and Investigation

At the One Ball Inn, the group split:

- Erelmor and n’Dong circled around the back, scoping out the Broken Fang drop site.
- Ballar, Ieuan and Enrak entered the gambling room after a tense greeting from Antoinne Cassan.

Inside, the players were laid out:

- Billy Hill, already drunk.
- Summer Quaresh, the diplomatic liaison for [[Clan Khaali]], joined by Eden Carrion (Voruun) and Jericho Beech (Temir).
- Three rough-looking strangers, one flanked by obvious muscle.

Ballar identified The Old Grey Pike: a hulking half-orc whose scowl could crack stone. He tried to leverage their shared [[The Powers That Be#Doro|Doro]] heritage, but the Pike spat his contempt, especially toward House Nirrell.

Meanwhile, outside, Erelmor went invisible and n’Dong hid as they watched Antoinne Cassan conduct a clandestine deal in the cellar. Two men reported a bungled drop — something about a dragonborn smashing whiskey barrels (n’Dong’s doing, of course). Cassan was livid, lambasting the Broken Fang’s reputation. The dealers slunk off to search the brewery, with Erelmor and n’Dong tailing them into the woods.

## Roll the Bones

Back inside, the game began. Six rounds of Pig. Stakes were high. 300 gold a round. Ballar had to win to avoid crippling debt to Doro.

The results:

- The Pike wins round one.
- The Pike again. Silent. Unshakable.
- Billy Hill, somehow.
- Summer Quaresh, flashing that smug Khaali grin.
- Summer again, tightening her grip on the pot.

Ballar sat on the edge of ruin. Only a final round win could save him.

The dice rattled.

And somehow… he won. A narrow, brutal victory.

But just as the sweat dried and the coins were clutched, the Pike smiled, his teeth like broken knives. He pushed his winnings forward and asked, low and cold:

“Double or nothing?”
