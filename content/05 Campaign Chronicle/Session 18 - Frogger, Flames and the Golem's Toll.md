# Session 18: Frogger, Flames and the Golem’s Toll

> [!abstract] Session summary
> The party crosses Rana Viam’s living time puzzle and is annihilated by the golem guarding the relic.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 18 |
| **Recorded** | 03 May 2026 at 21:35 |
| **Arc** | Rana Viam |

As the Defracturers stared into the strange chamber ahead, the chaos before them suddenly stopped. The clouds of creatures that had been darting in every direction — mephits, flying snakes, cockatrices, stirges and worse — were all dragged into perfect stillness, frozen in place as though time itself had been paused. It was as if the room were holding its breath, waiting to see which of them would move first.

Naturally, Ballar was the one to break the silence. He strode straight into the room, and the moment he did, the magic snapped back into motion. A flying snake slammed bodily into him with a sickening thud, and in an instant Ballar found himself back at the beginning of the chamber, as though the entire attempt had been rewound.

That was enough to make it clear what they were dealing with, though not enough to make them approach it sensibly. n’Dong, true to form, became almost completely paralyzed by indecision, choosing to stand and observe the moving creatures in the hope that some hidden pattern would reveal itself. Enrak, at the opposite extreme, hurled himself into the challenge with reckless confidence and reached the middle of the room before n’Dong had managed to take even a single meaningful step.

Meanwhile, Erelmor and Ieuan attempted something like cooperation, moving together for a time and trying to solve the flow of creatures as a pair. That effort ended abruptly when a stirge caught Erelmor off guard and sent him back to the beginning.

At the center of the room stood a plinth, and when Enrak reached it, he immediately did what Enrak does best: he pressed the button without much thought for consequences. A distinct sound rang out through the chamber, and for reasons he could not fully explain, Enrak suddenly felt safe.

That was enough for the others. Ballar, n’Dong, and Ieuan made their way to the middle in relatively short order, though Erelmor remained stranded at the entrance. As n’Dong continued to linger and study the patterns, the others launched themselves toward the final stretch.

Once again, Enrak sprinted ahead — but this time, just a little too carelessly. He stumbled into the path of a cockatrice and took a hit that seemed to drain the strength straight out of his body, the petrification creeping over him almost instantly. Ballar, thrown off by Enrak’s collision, was struck by a cockatrice as well, though he managed to resist the same fate.

The room spat them both back to the center. Enrak, however, returned as a stone statue.

For once, Ballar was the only one who seemed to care immediately. While Din whispered unhelpfully in his ear that perhaps this was a good time to take a long rest, Ballar instead threw himself into the absurd logistics of rescuing a petrified companion. He tried to work out whether Enrak could be dragged with block and tackle, wondered if he could somehow fit him into the bag of holding, and finally attempted to simply lift the stone man by brute force. Impressively, he managed at least to tip Enrak onto his side.

Erelmor arrived at the center just in time to witness this display of commitment, offered precisely no assistance, and moved on. Spotting the second plinth at the far end of the room, he briefly attempted to solve the problem from range by throwing a dagger at it, but his aim was well off, coming far closer to hitting one of the roaming creatures than the mechanism he intended.

Realizing no one else was going to solve Enrak’s predicament, Ballar caught up with the others and informed Ieuan. The druid then tried something entirely bizarre: he cast Moonbeam directly onto Enrak’s petrified body. Against all logic, and after dealing the helpless fire mage a respectable amount of damage, the spell actually reversed the petrification. Disaster narrowly avoided.

At last, the group stumbled their way through the final segment of the chamber and collapsed onto the five pressure plates at the back. As they did, the rear wall slid open, revealing a narrow corridor and another sealed chamber beyond. Ignoring Din’s increasingly sensible suggestion that they rest while they still could, the Defracturers pressed on.

Inside the next chamber stood a massive stone golem, dormant but imposing, wearing a pair of ornate bracers that immediately drew the group’s attention. Nearby was a small dais holding a scroll within a bowl, accompanied by a cryptic warning etched into its stand.

> ***What strength you clutch, will quickly be lost.***
> ***What power you yield, will increase your odds.***
> ***Feed me all you would spend in war,***
> ***And you'll be granted one chance more.***
> ***Empty hands may yet prevail -***
> ***But those who offer nothing, will fail.***

The group, naturally, focused all their energy on the golem, trying to make sense how it related to the warning in the least efficient ways possible. They handed it weapons. They placed things in its hands. Ballar, for reasons perhaps known only to him, tried putting a javelin into its mouth. When that achieved nothing, n’Dong decided to try an emerald instead. The golem remained inert.

Eventually, having exhausted these baffling possibilities, they returned to the dais. Ieuan took the scroll from the bowl. That was the trigger. The golem stirred to life as the bracers began to flicker with ancient power. A surge of stasis magic washed over the room, seizing at the party and demanding a toll. Each of them felt some invisible pressure urging them to surrender their strength.

Enrak, Ieuan, and n’Dong yielded willingly, gasping as magic and power drained from them. Ballar resisted at first, but eventually relented and let some of his strength flow away. Only Erelmor refused entirely, hoarding his power without hesitation.

When the effect ended, four green flames burned above the dais. Then the golem roared to life.

Ballar charged first, stepping into melee against the construct without hesitation. Erelmor attempted a different tactic, trying to reason with it through Dissonant Whispers, but the construct had no interest in negotiation. n’Dong, in what may have been the best decision of the encounter, mocked the golem by calling it a “shit transformer,” and somehow the insult landed with surprising force.

While Ballar and the others occupied the construct, Enrak and Ieuan turned their attention to the strange green flames, trying to work out what they represented. Their investigations were cut short when the golem brought one of its enormous fists down on Ballar with horrifying force, dropping him before the group could properly process what they had just seen.

Suddenly the encounter stopped being amusing.

Erelmor rushed in and tried to strip the bracers from the golem, managing to undo one before being hurled away. Ieuan revived Ballar. Enrak unleashed the power of his Circlet of Blasting. Erelmor hurled psychic blades in frustration, only to realize they were all but useless against the construct. Desperate, he sprinted to the dais and tried to surrender his power belatedly — but the chamber remained silent. That opportunity had passed.

Even with the group now fully engaged, they were hopelessly outmatched. The golem moved with methodical brutality, felling them one by one until the chamber fell quiet.

And then, somehow, they awoke. The Defracturers found themselves outside the golem’s chamber, bloodied, battered, but alive. Whatever magic governed the ruin had spared them a true death — or perhaps merely reset the challenge once again.

When Erelmor cautiously peeked back into the room, he saw one crucial difference. There were now only three green flames above the dais.

That was enough to make the lesson clear. Shaken and finally willing to listen, the group at last took Din’s advice and settled in for a long rest, knowing they would need every scrap of strength if they hoped to survive their next attempt.
