# Session 07: Coffee and Contraband

> [!abstract] Session summary
> Elmwood reveals his interest in Tace Nox, n’Dong uncovers a smuggling network and a cursed mask forces personal reckonings.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 7 |
| **Recorded** | 11 November 2025 at 22:36 |

## 9AM – Patience Wears Thin (Erelmor)

With the dawn barely settled and the rest of [[Somnolent]] still nursing hangovers or hang-ups, Erelmor set off toward the arcades, singularly focused on acquiring something a little more practical than the battered leathers he’d been wearing since Thistlewound. He found his mark in Zephir Tomes, a gnomish leatherworker whose shop had only just opened its shutters for the day.

What began as a civil conversation—helm versus bycoket, flexibility versus coverage — quickly became an increasingly bizarre negotiation, one where Erelmor seemed less interested in the armour's craftsmanship and more in its delivery time. Eventually, with a huff and 45 gold lighter, Erelmor secured a custom set of studded leather armour, to be completed in two to three days.

Business concluded, most would have left. Not Erelmor.

He simply turned, found a bench across from the shop, and began to stare. Quietly. Unblinking. Waiting.

It didn’t take long for Zephir’s comfort to wear thin. The gnome came out himself to request — politely but firmly — that Erelmor go stare somewhere else. With his social grace laid bare like a gutted rabbit, the rogue finally rose and wandered off to find Ballar, sulking, perhaps, or simply confused that time didn’t pass faster just because he wanted it to.

## 10AM – Unveiling the gambit (Ieuan)

For the better part of a day and a half, Ieuan had been doing his best to casually ask every tavern owner, barkeep, and wayward soul whether Elmwood had mentioned him. When that yielded no results, he finally did what he’d clearly wanted to do all along: he went to the Crooked Hat.

Navigating the arcane defenses of the eccentric wizard’s crooked tower — first the Seeing Door, then the disorienting, logic-defying staircase — Ieuan arrived once more in the presence of Elmwood, the ever-frazzled, ever-focused scholar of the [[The Sundering of Ardynia|Ardynian]] past.

Elmwood wasted no time. He launched into his concerns about the growing dangers around Ardynian ruins, the power they held, and how vital it was that the wrong people didn’t get their hands on the scrolls he believed lay buried within. Ieuan listened, an amused smile tugging at the corners of his mouth, realising almost immediately that the ruin Elmwood spoke of, Tace Nox, was one he'd already explored.

And yet, he said nothing.

Elmwood continued. He confessed he had once been a lead researcher at the Pale Seminary in [[The Powers That Be#Minir|Minir]], but had fled, disillusioned by the increasing influence of radical factions like the Broken Fang—people who sought power, not understanding. He had moved to Somnolent in secret, hoping to gain access to the ruins before his rivals could reach them. The notes he'd stolen, the misinformation he'd fed to his colleagues—it had all been a gambit, a desperate bid to protect something ancient.

What he needed now was a crew—fast, competent, and discreet. He offered the job to Ieuan, unaware he’d already completed part of the mission. Ieuan, to his credit, tried to keep the lie going. He chuckled about assembling a new team, downplayed the importance of his "back-up" crew, and offered vague reassurances. But the moment Elmwood mentioned the scrolls again—their potency, their responsibility—Ieuan’s mind flashed to Enrak juggling flaming cantrips while shouting about destiny. The druid’s mask cracked. He flinched. And Elmwood saw through it.

Silence followed.

The old man’s demeanour shifted—from kindly to cold. The pain of betrayal was evident. He demanded an explanation. He deserved one. Ieuan backpedalled. He admitted they’d explored Tace Nox, that he knew where the scroll was, and that he hadn’t acted alone. This was now his primary party, he insisted. He would return with the scroll—and with them.

Elmwood didn’t reply immediately. He merely nodded, disappointed but resolved.

The stakes had changed.

## 11AM – Brewed and branded (Ballar)

After a morning of quiet scheming, Ballar set out with a singular mission: *coffee.* His destination: Brewed Awakening, the trendiest caffeine cart in Somnolent, manned by the unflappable and perennially unimpressed Saffron.

An argument over cortados somehow turned into a philosophical debate on loyalty programs, which culminated in Ballar begrudgingly offering his actual flesh for a loyalty sigil. One “[[The Powers That Be#Doro|Doro]] Diplomat,” one “Ashwake,” and two of Saffron’s buns later, the tab was tallied — but just as Ballar reached the end of his patience, Erelmor appeared. Buzzing with new-coffee energy, the rogue ordered another drink, and Ieuan zoomed past requesting one to-go. Ballar picked up the tab for both, and earned a few more loyalty brands.

Caffeinated and clinging to sanity, the pair made their way to Balur Moonclaw, the tiefling blacksmith. Ballar, in a moment of self-indulgent bravado, snapped a chain in half with his bare hands—one Balur claimed had previously restrained an owlbear. With raised eyebrows, the smith presented a thicker one. This one held, and Ballar happily purchased it along with a pair of manacles.

Their testosterone display turned theatrical as Balur mentioned the fighting pits again, challenging Ballar to a future duel. The two squared off like a low-budget promotional poster — bulging arms, scowls, mutual respect hanging in the air like sweat.

Still flush with coffee and bravado, Ballar dragged the giddy Erelmor (now vibrating from his first caffeine experience) to the Doro Embassy, where he promptly demanded funds from Stacii to infiltrate Alaaric’s dice game.

Stacii, ever the administrative predator, slid over a contract dripping in fine print. “We don’t just hand out 250 gold,” she said sweetly. Ballar signed without reading, then whisked Erelmor off for a celebratory tour of the embassy’s gym for some flexing and a sauna.

## 1PM – The barrel and the bricks (n’Dong)

Still soggy and shaken from his ill-advised whiskey barrel ride, n’Dong emerged at the docks trying to piece together where exactly he’d landed. The shack nearby looked decrepit at first glance, but his sharp eyes caught the glint of new hinges and recently turned soil. A recently disturbed barrel concealed a set of keys, which opened the shack to reveal something far more sinister.

Inside, he found crates of supplies… and clingfilm-wrapped bricks of something suspicious. Driven by curiosity (and a touch of madness), n’Dong dipped a finger into one and rubbed it on his gums.

A mistake. A catastrophic, nerve-shredding, gut-twisting mistake.

The pain knocked him to the floor — convulsing, screaming, his brain reduced to a static of agony. Ten minutes passed before he staggered upright, pale and drenched in sweat.

Somewhere in the haze, he found a manifest of drop-offs, linking multiple locations:

- *One Ball Inn (Somnolent) - 4 x Barrel whiskey,*
- *The Scythian tree (Orvak) - 2 x Rations, 4 x weapons/armour*
- *Fighting pits (Voruun) - Agony, Powdered Power, Dragon's Dandruff*
- *Ashka's den (Khaali) - Unicorn wax, Ember Berries*

Before he could process it, he heard hoofbeats. Acting on pure instinct, n’Dong submerged himself like a swamp creature, hiding beneath the dock.

A halfling merchant approached. Curious, unbothered, and only mildly concerned after having a rock hurled at his horse, he soon met n’Dong — who revealed himself like a half-drowned monster from a folk tale. The halfling introduced himself as Theo Dodds, a travelling trader

Theo was heading to Somnolent, and after some hesitation (and grabbing a barrel of whiskey), n’Dong decided to join him. Perhaps the best way forward was to follow the flow of illicit goods… back to where it all began.

## 2PM – Secrets and schemes

By mid-afternoon, the Squishy Boys reconvened at the Drowsy Coin, one lizard short of a full party. Enrak was already deep in his cups, holding court like a one-man carnival while Ieuan paced behind him, eyes wide with worry, quietly praying he hadn't said too much — or worse, said something true.

Ballar, all poise and presence, scanned the tavern once and decided this was no place for secrets. Calling over the ever-jovial Callum Redmayne, he demanded privacy. Though Callum noted Enrak already had a room, Ballar waved him off with an upturned palm and ordered the finest room available, complete with drinks — vermouths and cortados, naturally. As they made their way upstairs, he left one final instruction: if a lizard man showed up, send him up immediately.

In their private chamber, the chaos of the tavern melted away — replaced by a tangled conversation of half-truths, tension, and too many threads to pull cleanly.

Ieuan, working subtly, managed to retrieve the Ardynian scroll from a very drunk Enrak, stashing it safely before the others noticed. He pressed the group to focus on the ruins, on Elmwood, on the mysteries buried beneath Vaelond that could change their world.

Ballar, however, had more tangible ambitions. Dice games and heists. He pitched a plan to raid Alaaric Hammerfall’s safe while distracting him at the One Ball Inn gambling tables. But his motivations were layered — carefully concealed beneath bravado. He held his cards tight, manipulating the conversation while deflecting attention from his own inquiries.

The mention of the One Ball Inn stirred Erelmor, who revealed he was tracking a man, the Old Grey Pike, and suspected he might be tied to this shadowy gambling ring.

At last, n’Dong appeared, drenched from his barrel-bound escapade but unbothered. He presented a list found at the drop site—various contraband and substances, most identifiable save one: Agony, which they realized n’Dong himself had imbibed during his infiltration.

As plans and theories flew across the room, Callum Redmayne poked his head in with perfect timing — and a classic side quest. There was something strange in the basement, and he’d appreciate the group looking into it.

Resigned to distraction, the group agreed — if only to clear their heads.

## 4pm — The Mask in the Dark

In the musty cellar of the Drowsy Coin, the party’s search began with a distinct lack of subtlety. Crates were kicked, dust was disturbed, and spirits were stirred.

Ballar, perhaps trying to move things along, landed a firm kick to a pile of crates—and his foot struck something unnatural. A mask, old and brittle, slid free from the wreckage.

And then the world shifted.

Each of them was suddenly, terrifyingly alone — but not in body. In memory.

- n’Dong saw Prodosius, grinning with mirthless spite, boasting of slaughtering his parents and warning his death would soon follow.
- Ieuan watched a wood elf poisoner, whispering of betrayal and treachery, crowing, *“You always knew it was me.”*
- Enrak stood before Ashka, goddess of fire and chaos, who gazed into him and asked simply: *“Who are you?”*
- Ballar was confronted by his mother, eyes burning with disdain as she spat, *“You are no son of mine.”*
- Erelmor saw his wife, dying and broken on the floor, her blood pooling around her as she looked up and begged: *“Why didn't you save us?”*

The illusions fractured. A wave pulsed through the basement, knocking the party back into themselves — only two figures remained: Ballar’s mother, and Erelmor’s wife, now apparitions of fury and grief.

The fight was brutal — not in blows traded, but in what it cost. The spectres tore into their minds and bodies. Ballar, whose strength had long been a pillar of pride, felt his muscles wither in real time, sapped by the very image of his mother’s disappointment. And still, they fought — cutting down the ghosts with spells and steel, until at last, the echoes were silenced.

What remained was the mask — still and silent.

As the group caught their breath, n’Dong approached the mask with rare solemnity. Without prompting, without thought, he recited a forgotten prayer — a supplication to Verei, the Shattered Mask, whose broken visage watches over secrets and spirits.

The curse lifted. The mask fell inert. Without ceremony, n’Dong pocketed it.

No one questioned it.

The group returned upstairs, shaken. Ballar bolted to his room, hollowed out by the vision of his mother’s scorn, strength draining from his frame. Erelmor was wild-eyed, haunted by the bloodied plea of the woman he could not forget. The others followed, each retreating to silence in their own way.

Whatever had happened in the basement wasn’t just a haunting. It was a reckoning. And none of them had walked away unscathed.
