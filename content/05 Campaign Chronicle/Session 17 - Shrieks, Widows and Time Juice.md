# Session 17: Shrieks, Widows and Time Juice

> [!abstract] Session summary
> After an owlbear attack and an ogre rescue, the party reaches Rana Viam and hears Saint Vaelen’s warning.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 17 |
| **Recorded** | 22 April 2026 at 19:23 |
| **Arc** | Rana Viam |

After the misery of the previous day’s travel, the Defracturers finally settled into the secluded camp Ieuan had found for them. The place was defensible, sheltered, and about as close to peace as the road seemed willing to grant them. The group spent some time tending to their horses, all of whom had endured the horrors of the last day with varying degrees of grace. While every mount responded well enough to food, water, and rest, it was impossible not to notice that the two sturdy beasts, Bracken and Cobb, seemed the least bothered by anything, as though hardship simply rolled off them.

As the camp settled into its rotation of watches and rest, Ballar was eager to trance first. Unfortunately, he did not find silence there. The soul within his Ring of Mind Shielding, the one calling itself Din, interrupted him again, pressing for answers. It wanted to know where they were, where they were going, and why.

Ballar, unsurprisingly, had questions of his own. Who was Din? What did he want? And, perhaps most importantly, why should Ballar care in the first place? Din answered more plainly than before. He wanted a body. He believed Ballar might be the sort of man capable of finding one for him, and in return he would help guide the group through the ruins ahead, at least where he could. Ballar did not seem particularly impressed by this bargain, and the two eventually reached an uneasy pause. They would shelve the conversation until the promised help was actually needed.

Later, while Erelmor entered his own trance, things went much worse. His rest became, as it so often did, a descent into nightmare. But with Cackle Fever still clinging to him, the nightmare did not remain private. Instead, he burst into wild, unhinged laughter for half a minute, the sound carrying through the trees and shredding the calm of the camp.

Even the best resting place could not protect them from that.

It was not long before the laughter drew the attention of two hulking owlbears. Worse still, as the creatures closed in, Ieuan and n’Dong also succumbed to the shrieks, leaving half the party helpless with laughter just as the camp came under attack. Ballar heard the chaos and, in a moment of grim tactical assessment, apparently decided that Enrak could probably handle one owlbear alone. This proved optimistic. Within moments, the beast had buried its claws into the fire mage’s very soft and vulnerable flesh.

Then Ballar did something unexpected. Drawing on strange and specific knowledge from his youth, apparently his family had kept owlbears in the Novis family zoo, he somehow managed to intimidate one of the creatures so completely that it fled. It was a ridiculous and yet extremely effective display.

By then, Ieuan and n’Dong had shaken off their hysterics and rushed to help Enrak. But the true hero of the moment was Bracken, Enrak’s broad, sturdy, deeply unglamorous horse. Seeing his rider in danger, the great beast took the owlbear head-on, buying the group the time they needed to kill it and, more importantly, to keep Enrak alive.

The rest of the long rest passed in an uneasy half-silence. No one truly relaxed after that. Still, when morning came, Ieuan and n’Dong found themselves free of the shrieks, their bodies having finally shaken off the disease. Ballar, recognizing how much of a liability Erelmor remained under its effects, used Lay on Hands to cure him, though the effort left him with even fewer healing reserves for the road ahead.

The party resumed their journey. They had not gone far before they came upon another grim roadside scene: a broken cart, the corpse of an elf, the corpse of a horse that appeared to have been bitten, and a clear trail of blood leading onward. Ballar took point and quickly spotted the source of the danger—a pair of ogres lurking near a house ahead. With uncharacteristic good sense, he immediately sent his own horse running from the danger.

The party advanced, hearing screams from inside the house just as a third ogre emerged from the shadows. Battle began in earnest. The group fought well at first, and n’Dong in particular acted decisively, using his psychic shove to place himself between one of the ogres and the woman trapped inside the house.

But then one of the creatures landed a strike on Ballar so brutal it nearly took his head from his body. The mood changed instantly.

Resolve gave way to fear and caution. n’Dong disengaged and hid, while the rest of the Defracturers abandoned any thought of a clean engagement and instead began taking potshots from range. The strategy worked well enough to bring down two of the ogres, but the last was no fool. It crashed through the house and turned its attention on the woman inside.

Ballar threw himself back into the fray, casting Compelled Duel to drag the ogre’s attention onto himself, while n’Dong pulled the woman through a window to safety. This was heroic. Unfortunately, n’Dong then undercut the moment entirely by casually explaining to the deeply injured and traumatized woman that her husband had been “completely destroyed,” apparently without realizing the sacrifice he had made to keep the woman alive.

Thankfully, Ieuan had better instincts. He quickly healed the woman, then rejoined the fight and struck the ogre viciously, leaving it tottering on the brink of death. Ballar stepped in and delivered the final blow. The rescued woman introduced herself as Mireya. She had been traveling to Hushvale with her husband, planning to establish a gambling hall there, but his sudden and thoroughly unpleasant death had understandably cast that dream into doubt.

At this point, an old friend appeared. Eula, absolutely delighted to add some Ogres to her cache of bodies, provided them with 75 coins for the three. The group questioned her as intensely as they felt they could, gaining an understanding that the coins let Eula know when a murder was committed nearby, and with that vanished.

The Defracturers began to discuss what they should do with the coins, whether to take them, spend them, keep them at the bastion, or to leave them in Rana Viam. When they mentioned Rana Viam, Mireya visibly paled. She had heard enough rumours about the ruins—how people could become trapped within for impossible lengths of time—to immediately ask the most practical question anyone had raised all day:

“Who is going to look after the horses?” It was an excellent point.

The party agreed to let Mireya travel with them and tend the horses while they explored the ruins. Ballar was convinced, if not delighted, by her competence almost immediately, especially when she managed to get Kingcup eating from her palm with insulting ease. Bracken took on the additional burden of hauling the cart and the husband’s body, while Mireya rode Cobb alongside Ieuan. Just outside Rana Viam, the party paused long enough to give the dead man a proper burial. Only then did they finally approach the ruin itself.

At the entrance, the group encountered a riddle and, whether through intelligence or sheer luck, solved it quickly enough that even they seemed unsure which of the two deserved the credit. Inside, they took stock of the initial layout. A collection of bodies to the right immediately suggested danger, and the party sensibly chose to avoid that problem for the time being. Erelmor, still not fully trusting the space, went one step further and turned invisible.

They checked the central corridor first, where they found a basin containing a familiar green residue. Ieuan immediately recognized it as the same “Time Juice” they had encountered in Tace Nox. Ballar tried, and failed, to solve the adjacent door with brute force, so they moved on.

The left corridor led them to what looked like a bedroom, one that Erelmor suspected had once belonged to a chwinga. The room was cluttered with arcane paraphernalia: alchemical devices, notes, books, and one very notable flimsy wall.

Ballar, being Ballar, chose not to inspect it carefully. Instead, he hurled himself at it with all the subtlety of a siege engine, smashing through in a single leap like some unholy combination of the Kool-Aid Man and a charging war elephant. Beyond, the group discovered a more deliberate workspace, containing a desk with a single stone upon it and a chest nearby.

Ieuan examined the stone and, through instinct and a growing familiarity with [[The Sundering of Ardynia|Ardynian]] devices, deciphered the runes enough to activate it. What followed was a recorded message from Saint Vaelen, and it dumped a terrifying amount of information into the room. The message confirmed that the Saints of Elgira were not merely myth, but real figures—and deeply divided ones at that. Vaelen feared for his life, caught between Marra, whose hunger for war threatened to consume everything, Desmon, whose obsession with power seemed equally dangerous, and Erith, whose detachment was perhaps worst of all.

While the others absorbed that revelation, n’Dong turned his attention to the alchemical workstation and, with a practiced hand no doubt informed by years of poisoncraft, managed to extract two more vials of Time Juice from the equipment in addition to the beaker they had already found.

Only then did the group return to the bodies they had avoided earlier. n’Dong examined them and determined they had likely died one to two weeks ago. More importantly, they bore the unmistakable sigil of the Broken Fang.

At that point, still invisible, Erelmor knocked on a nearby door and heard the unmistakable sound of a troll on the other side. The party wasted no time. They fled back to the centre corridor, where n’Dong poured some Time Juice into the basin. Arcane green light shimmered through the adjoining door in a web-like pattern, and the passage slowly opened.

Beyond lay one of the strangest sights the Defracturers had yet seen. The room ahead was filled with motion—mephits, snakes, cockatrices, stirges, imps, all zig-zagging chaotically across the chamber in every direction.

The whole thing looked like a living puzzle. Or, as Erelmor put it with perfect clarity: “Looks like Frogger.”
