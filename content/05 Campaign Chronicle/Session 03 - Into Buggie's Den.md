# Session 03: Into Buggie's Den

> [!abstract] Session summary
> A tense parley with Buggie reveals new enemies before the party enters the Ardynian ruins beneath Thistlewound.

## Into the Bugbear’s Den

In the damp throat of the wilds, beneath the looming stone of Thistlewound and the weight of unseen eyes, the party stood face-to-face with a monster.

**Buggie**, towering and scarred, was not the frothing warbeast they expected, but a warlord weary of blood. Behind him, goblins and bugbears watched in silence—not the frenzied swarm that had assaulted [[Somnolent]], but sullen, displaced kin, licking old wounds beneath mossy banners.

The tension was immediate and knife-sharp. The party, never ones to flinch, **openly admitted to killing Ryzdak**, Buggie’s commander, and **Erelmor** in particular seemed to savour the admission. Buggie, to his credit, did not rise to anger. Instead, he spoke of what truly mattered: **a coin** Ryzdak carried—an insurance policy.

Feigning ignorance, the party offered a deal. In return for information on the **Crow** who had hired them, they would help recover the coin. But patience was wearing thin. Words stretched taut. Finally, an accord was struck.

**Buggie would give them everything he had**: names, fragments, and a **document** smuggled at the Crow’s request—her last order before she disappeared. The failure to capture Ambassador Prusset meant, Buggie admitted with cold certainty, *they would likely never see her again.*

With all eyes watching, **Erelmor clumsily fumbled the handoff**, forcing **Ballar** to complete the transaction. As his fingers closed around the object, he realized the truth: this was no ordinary trinket. It was a **ceremonial coin**, minted in **[[The Powers That Be#Doro|Doro]]**, bearing the unmistakable crest of **House Bromere**.

Before they could leave, a **goblin warchanter demanded retribution** for her sister’s death. **Erelmor**, quick-witted even under threat, wove a half-truth—claiming they saw her die, yes, but not by their hand. The lie held. Barely.

As they turned to leave, **n’Dong**, silent till now, **pressed Buggie about a name from his past—Prodosius**. The bugbear's answer was chilling. His tribe had **fled [[The Powers That Be#Minir|Minir]]** to escape the Broken Fang, a ruthless force growing bolder, expanding from their borderland camp near **[[Clan Erzhin]]**. Of Prodosius, Buggie knew little—but enough to stoke a fire in n’Dong’s heart.

## Putting the pieces together

Smoke still clung to their clothes as the party emerged from the goblin hideout, the echoes of parlay with **Buggie the bugbear warlord** lingering in the air. The uneasy truce held, but beneath the surface—suspicion festered.

As the group trudged through the underbrush, **Ieuan’s jaw tightened**, eyes fixed on **n’Dong**. Without warning, he pulled the towering dragonborn aside, voice low and sharp.

**n’Dong didn’t flinch.** Instead, he exhaled a truth that hung heavy in the forest air. He had once been **enslaved by Prodosius**, a bugbear warlord whose name still made his fists curl. He hadn’t joined this quest for coin or glory. He’d seen the word *bugbear* in that crude goblin note and followed it like a bloodhound, knowing vengeance might lie at the end of the trail. Trust, once strained, began to realign—though not fully heal.

With spell slots empty, the party finally took some time to breathe — and think. They turned over what they had learnt like bones in a fire, watching the shadows shift and dance, and piece by bitter piece, a story began to form.

Theories became conviction as **Ieuan**, **Enrak**, **Ballar**, **Erelmor**, and **n’Dong** laid the web bare:

- Anthea Bloodclot didn’t die by fate. She was silenced.
- The [[Perfidian Faith|Church of Elgira]], threatened by her influence and unity, whispered the command.
- And it was Prusset, the [[The Powers That Be#Perfid|Perfid]] ambassador, who gave the order—using a shadowy Orvaki faction called the [[Rootbound]] to do the deed.

The assassination of Anthea was not just a murder—it was a **betrayal of the Five Clans**, a scalpel wound to their fragile alliance. Now, at least one of her hidden **Crows** was calling in old debts, with **Prusset** in her sights.

## Into the dark

Before them loomed the sealed stone threshold of **Thistlewound**, the ruins whispering secrets older than the clans themselves. *"Call Lights Out"* The words had lingered since Ieuan had first deciphered the ancient riddle, and now, as twilight clung to the edges of the forest, the moment had come.

There was hesitation. Some advised caution. Others waited for a sign.

**Erelmor** did not.

With the recklessness of a man chasing ghosts, he stepped into the dark without waiting for his allies. The others followed, some with blades drawn, some with prayers muttered. **Ballar**, bringing up the rear, exhaled a long-suffering sigh that echoed faintly into the depths.

The air shifted the moment they crossed the threshold. **Suffocating pressure**, oppressive and humid, settled over them like a damp shroud. The ruins pulsed with a sickly **green light**, and something unnatural stirred in the walls.

They crept through narrow halls when a **Chwinga**—a flickering, fey-like spirit—darted across their path and vanished through solid stone. A harmless omen, perhaps. But to nerves already taut, it felt like a warning.

In the first chamber, a flash of light drew their eyes to a grim statue of an **[[The Sundering of Ardynia|Ardynian]] soldier**. A staff vanished from its grasp, swallowed by unseen forces. Instinct screamed at them to turn back. Instead, they doubled down.

**Ballar**, growing suspicious, cast forth a **brown bear** from his Bag of Rust Tricks, using the creature as a trap tester.

Meanwhile, **Ieuan** shifted into the form of a rat to scout ahead. Neither beast found any traps—but what they did find was worse: **bodies**. Dozens. Strewn across rooms and corridors, decaying in robes of faded glory. The stink of **death** clung to the stone like moss.

And yet, amidst this desecrated sanctum, **Erelmor** looked up at the jeweled eyes of the statue and thought, *I want those.* He attempted to scale it—failed—and fell with a graceless thud. Rat-Ieuan bit him on the neck in reprimand.

Their path led them to what remained of a **living quarter**, aged and dust-choked but intact. Among cracked tomes and shattered furniture, they uncovered:

- An **Orb of Time**
- Books cataloguing **creatures of immense lifespans**—liches, dragons, phoenixes.
- And most damning of all, a **discarded letter** penned by **Anthea Bloodclot** herself, revealing she had once entered these ruins and encountered strange puzzles and deadlier things still.

Anthea's letter confirmed the origin of the ruins. **Ardynian.** The name alone was enough to chill **n’Dong**, who confessed in a rare moment of openness: *It was ruins like these… Ardynian… that twisted me into what I am.* The others could only wonder what had once happened to him in the dark.

In a tomb-like chamber, the bear—still loyally blundering ahead—**kicked open a door**, revealing three monstrous **giant centipedes**. Acting quickly, Erelmor turned the ground into chaos, flinging a sack of **ball bearings** to slow their advance.

It didn’t work.

The pedes simply climbed the walls and descended upon the party from above.

The fight was fast and vicious, but the party stood strong. **Ballar**, ever the finisher, struck the final blow—again.

While Erelmor grudgingly gathered up his scattered bearings, the others investigated:

- **Ballar and Enrak** retrieved more **healing potions** from near the sarcophagi.
- **n’Dong** found three **jade crystals** embedded in an altar, which he pocketed swiftly—drawing a glare from Erelmor.
- A **mosaic** confirmed the earlier note’s clue: the solution to the ruin’s puzzle lay in creating an **arrow of light** pointing toward the altar.

**Ballar**, seized by a sudden burst of industrious madness, ran through the ruins **tearing sconces from the walls**, hoping to piece together the arrow manually.

While Ballar made his frantic rounds, **n’Dong, Ieuan, and Enrak** discovered the real puzzle: an ancient mechanism already half-triggered by the bear’s heavy paws.

Panic set in. Had they already failed?

They regrouped, tensions high. Ballar scolded his bear. The others argued. Then, without warning, **Erelmor and n’Dong** stepped forward — and triggered the puzzle prematurely.

Green light **flickered** violently through the chamber. The ruins screamed. From the shadows emerged a **skeletal warhorse**, its hooves crackling with unnatural energy. The battle was fierce, but fleeting. The party, galvanized by fear, fell upon it. **Ballar**, resolute and righteous, delivered the final strike—again.

Not wanting to tempt fate a second time, they approached the puzzle with care. **Ieuan** scratched diagrams into the dust, frantically testing combinations. **Ballar** declared it impossible. **Erelmor**, tired of the noise, **found the answer**.

With a shudder and a groan, the puzzle accepted their resolution. The green light pulsed, focused, and coalesced into a swirling **portal above the altar**.

It opened. But what lay beyond?
