# Session 08: The Weight of Secrets

> [!abstract] Session summary
> Romayne forces the truth into the open as the party pursues Ambassador Prusset through Somnolent.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 8 |
| **Recorded** | 01 December 2025 at 20:36 |
| **Arc** | The Prusset affair |

The air was heavy in the Drowsy Coin. The encounter in the cellar still clung to the party like soot. As the day wore on toward evening, each member tried in their own way to pretend things were normal. But normal had long since left [[Somnolent]].

Upstairs, Ballar was alone, still reeling. With slow, deliberate motions he slid into his armour, each buckle and strap a ritual, a shield against the voice of his mother still echoing in his skull. As he adjusted the last piece, something strange happened — the ring on his finger whispered to him.

It wasn’t a voice in his head. It wasn’t comfort. It was something darker. Ballar tried to speak back. No reply. He removed the ring. Waited. Put it back on.

Still silence.

His eyes narrowed. Was it cursed? Was it watching?

He said nothing to the others.

## The Summons

Downstairs, Callum Redmayne approached the rest of the group — Ieuan, n’Dong, Erelmor, and Enrak (who, wisely or not, was left to his drink). A messenger had come from Romayne Drayton, requesting their presence immediately.

When they went to collect Ballar, they were met only with a sharp rebuke through the door. “I’m not coming. Don’t tell Romayne anything.”

A decision was made: Enrak would stay at the Drowsy Coin, where he could do the least damage. The rest would go, minus Ballar, with Erelmor deciding to wait outside Drayton’s office, wary of saying something unwise.

Inside Drayton’s study, the tone was different than before. The usual calm of Somnolent’s headman had turned quietly sharp, like a blade beneath silk. He motioned for Ieuan and n’Dong to sit.

They chose their words carefully — but not carefully enough. They claimed ignorance. The goblin attack, they said, was random, opportunistic. Nothing more. Romayne didn’t buy it for a second.

He reminded them of the note, the one the party had found on the goblin commander that read: No kill, just scare. Get Pruzet. Crow want alive. Buggie.

Their bluff shattered. Calm but firm, Romayne Drayton demanded the truth. All of it. No more lies, no more delays.

n’Dong, with an uncharacteristic show of openness, handed over the silver coin. Ieuan followed, revealing what they had found: proof that Alistair Prusset was involved in the murder of Anthea Bloodclot. Drayton turned the coin over in his hands. His expression darkened.

“This… is a Bromere favour coin,” he said quietly. “If this is what it looks like… it implicates [[The Powers That Be#Doro|Doro]] in the attempted abduction of a [[The Powers That Be#Perfid|Perfid]] ambassador.” The room went still. Romayne demanded the proof of Perfid and Prusset's involvement in Anthea Bloodclot's murder.

With Romayne’s words still echoing in their heads, Ieuan and n’Dong left Drayton’s office in a hurry — tails tucked, truth reluctantly spilled. Outside, Erelmor fell into step without question, sensing the urgency but not yet knowing the reason. There was no time for explanation. They needed one thing: Ballar.

Back at the Drowsy Coin, Ballar had emerged from his self-imposed exile. Recovered, at least in posture, he greeted Ieuan with surprising levity. They spoke like old friends discussing a pleasant evening ahead, not like conspirators standing on a political powder keg. Plans for the night took shape quickly: Ballar would attend the dice game at the One Ball Inn, while Erelmor and Enrak — the proverbial loose cannons — would attempt to infiltrate Alaaric’s office and crack the mysterious safe.

With the air seemingly clear, Ieuan pivoted casually, asking for the documents Ballar had. The paladin handed them over with minimal fuss, trusting that someone else might better make sense of them. With the proof Romayne needed now in hand, Ieuan and n’Dong slipped out once more. Neither of them noticed Erelmor, quietly slipping into the shadows behind them.

As they reached the street, Erelmor watched in surprise as the two split ways — Ieuan heading back toward Drayton’s office, and n’Dong turning toward the One Ball Inn. Suspicion flared. He tailed n’Dong, unwilling to risk the dragonborn stumbling into The Old Grey Pike unprepared.

The One Ball Inn was a different beast in daylight. Less den of intrigue, more forgotten corner of Somnolent’s underbelly. Inside, the bar was nearly empty. Only the town drunk, Billy Hill, and the inn’s landlord, Antoinne Cassan, kept the place breathing. n’Dong, casual and composed, ordered a drink and pressed Antoinne for information about the dice game. But Antoinne, slick as oil and twice as hard to catch, offered little but cold stone and empty words.

As n’Dong turned to leave, Billy Hill wobbled in his seat and beckoned. Slurring through his beard, he offered information for beer. n’Dong obliged whilst, outside, Erelmor crouched beneath the window and listened intently. Billy, now thoroughly rehydrated with ale, managed to string together a few slurred truths:

The dice game runs two to three times a week. The game of choice is Pig — simple, risky, and perfect for gambling favours. Once, he’d caught Antoinne doing something shady. He couldn’t quite recall what, only that it was the kind of thing a man doesn’t talk about unless he wants to end up in the river. It wasn’t much — but it was enough. n’Dong left without fanfare. Erelmor melted into the street behind him, his thoughts now knotted with more than just The Pike.

## Ballads, balconies and bad decisions

Back at the Drowsy Coin, tensions flared not from threats or omens — but from song. Ballar Novis sat at a table, drink untouched, his jaw tight and his knuckles white. Across the room, Ersil Loquella, the blonde bard with a silver tongue and dangerous charm, once again raised his voice to the delight of the patrons. This time, however, he named names. Ballar’s name.

The paladin’s glare could have split stone. As soon as the performance ended, Ballar approached Ersil with all the warmth of a drawn blade, inviting him to his room for a private drink. Ever the entertainer, Ersil accepted with a gracious bow and a suspiciously curious smile.

The moment the door closed, the act dropped. Ballar lunged, seizing the bard by the throat and dangling him over the second-storey balcony, eyes wild with fury. “How do you know my name?” he growled.

Ersil, remarkably composed despite the precarious situation, gave a simple answer: “Enrak told me.”

That didn’t help. Rage boiling over, Ballar threatened to drop him, demanding he never sing about him again. But Ersil only smiled faintly and misty stepped from Ballar’s grip — reappearing inside the room, untouched. The tension snapped. Ballar swung, landing a solid hit across Ersil’s face.

A mistake. With a wry grin, the bard whispered an incantation and cast Tasha’s Hideous Laughter. In moments, the iron-clad paladin was on the floor, shaking, giggling, convulsing, a wretched chorus of laughter exploding from his lungs against his will. Ersil left him there, humming to himself. Downstairs, Ballar could hear it start again: “Enrak! Enrak! The brazen flame!”

Ballar didn’t wait for the encore. Once the spell wore off, he stormed out, red-faced and seething, heading straight for the Doro consulate with the fury of a man thoroughly humiliated by rhyme.

Meanwhile, blissfully unaware of the lyrical assassination and subsequent balcony assault, Enrak was enjoying his growing celebrity, soaking in the praise like a dry sponge. His moment of peace was broken, however, by the arrival of a tired and frantic woman. She introduced herself as Rowena — wife to Terrick, the ranger who had accompanied the party to Thistlewound.

“He left four hours ago to meet you,” she said, barely holding herself together. “He said he was meeting you before going to see Romayne. I haven’t seen him since.” Enrak blinked.

“Terrick?” he asked, confused. “Never heard of him.”

Rowena stared. She explained again—patiently at first, then with rising frustration. Enrak remained utterly nonplussed, offering the most unhelpful reassurance imaginable. “I’m sure it’s fine. Don’t worry.”

Rowena slapped him. Hard. Then she left, furious and heartbroken. Enrak stood there a moment longer, processing nothing, then returned to his drink. Probably still humming his own theme song.

## The Ambassador’s Escape

While chaos played out everywhere else — bards thrown from balconies, wives begging for lost husbands, and plying drunks with booze for info — Ieuan stayed the course.

With the Perfidian documents in hand, he made his way alone to Romayne Drayton, the only one still treating the threat of civil war with the gravity it deserved. Behind closed doors, Ieuan laid bare the truth: proof of Perfid’s involvement in Anthea Bloodclot’s assassination. Romayne’s expression barely shifted, but the lines of tension deepened on his face.

“Perfid is pulling Prusset from Somnolent. They claim it’s due to a lack of protection. But now? We know it’s a cover—they’re hiding their involvement.” He leaned forward, tone sharpening.

“Stop him from leaving. Do not kill him. But if he escapes — you and your party will be held responsible for obstructing justice.” The weight of that sentence settled hard on Ieuan’s shoulders.

Ieuan wasted no time. He rushed back to the Drowsy Coin, gathered everyone he could find — Enrak, n’Dong, Erelmor — and made for the Doro Consulate to retrieve Ballar. They stopped once. For cortado-related bickering at Brewed Awakening. It was brief. Barely 10 minutes. It'd be fine.

Inside the consulate, they found Ballar — fresh from a sauna, glowing with calm that vanished the moment the group started loudly declaring their intent to arrest the Perfid ambassador. Stacii, Ballar’s eternally unimpressed colleague, raised a brow. Ballar nearly screamed. Slamming on his armor with record speed, he marched them out before they could say anything worse in front of more witnesses.

No sooner had they hit the street than they spotted Alistair Prusset, slipping through the town’s outer edges. The chase began immediately, both sides caught off guard. Prusset, desperate, turned and cast Slow, catching half the group. But Ieuan, unfazed, wild shaped into an elk, letting Ballar leap onto his back before charging after the ambassador like a battering ram.

Just as they closed the distance, Prusset blinked—shifting into the Ethereal Plane, disappearing from view. But the party had a wild card: Erelmor, and his Hag’s Eye amulet. He locked on to the flickering spirit of Prusset beyond the veil and shouted his location.

The pursuit twisted through back alleys, shifting planes, and cramped houses. Prusset popped in and out of existence, his Spirit Guardian tearing into anyone who got too close — dropping n’Dong and battering Ballar into bloodied fury.

Ballar, through grit and desperation, revived n’Dong and lunged to grab Prusset one last time. And then, from behind, a crack of force. Ieuan, in a panic, cast Thunderwave, ostensibly to help.

The result? Ballar was blasted backward, smashed into a wooden fence, the wind knocked clean from his lungs. His grip on Prusset gone. The ambassador slipped free once more.

Whether out of fear, instinct, or error the — blow might have cost them everything.

And as Prusset vanished into the Ethereal again, flickering like a dying flame, the party stood scattered, injured, and breathless — the stakes now higher than ever, and the consequences closing in fast.
