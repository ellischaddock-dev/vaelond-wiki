# Session 16: Cackles, Corpses and Catastrophe

> [!abstract] Session summary
> Bandits, Eula, stirges and Cackle Fever turn the journey towards Rana Viam into a disastrous retreat.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Session** | 16 |
| **Recorded** | 08 April 2026 at 17:03 |
| **Arc** | The road to Rana Viam |

Before the road called them onward once again, the Defracturers took a moment to bring some order to the growing machinery of their bastion. Their instructions were clear enough, if not exactly ambitious across the board. The workshops were tasked with preparing uniforms, the libraries were directed to begin research into the loyal crows, and the sanctuary was, for now, left entirely idle. With those orders settled, the group turned their focus back to the road and to the looming mystery of Rana Viam.

The journey resumed under a lingering shadow. Their recent encounter with the shambling mound had shaken them more than most were willing to admit, and perhaps because of that, they chose to press on without taking even a short rest. If the road ahead promised more danger, then speed seemed preferable to lingering weakness. That decision was tested before long.

As they approached a farm along the roadside, the party was ambushed by a group of bandits. The attack came quickly, but the Defracturers were in little mood for being delayed by common cutthroats. Though caught by surprise, they responded with ruthless efficiency and made short work of their attackers.

As ever, Erelmor was the first to the captain’s pockets. He found 50 gold pieces, but immediately noticed something deeply wrong about them. They bore the same reddish hue as the cursed coins they had encountered before — the same sort that heralded the appearance of the strange hooded woman. The group tossed the gold to the ground and began debating what to do. That was invitation enough.

The hooded woman appeared as suddenly and unnervingly as before, stepping into the scene with the calm confidence of someone arriving precisely where she expected to be. This time, she introduced herself as Eula. Without hesitation, she doubled the value of the coins in exchange for the bodies of the fallen bandits, treating the arrangement as though it were the most natural transaction in the world. This did not sit well with everyone.

Ballar, offended both as a paladin and as a man with some still-functioning sense of the sanctity of the dead, challenged the arrangement. He bristled at the idea of corpses being reduced to mere currency, especially when no proper rites or dignity would follow. The party pressed Eula for answers, but she was careful and maddeningly vague. All she would admit was that she was taking the bodies back to her base, and that she would be willing to pay well if they continued to provide her with fresh work.

Ballar, refusing to accept this quietly, attempted to ride off with one of the corpses in open defiance of her. Eula responded instantly, Misty Stepping across the space between them, plucking the body straight from his horse, and vanishing through a portal before any of them could properly react.

In the end, Erelmor came to the conclusion that money was money, and pocketed the reward without further moral struggle.

The party then made their way to the nearby farm, where they were greeted by the farmer Cobb — a fact which amused Ieuan, given his horse shared the name. Cobb was more than pleased to hear that the local bandits had been dealt with and offered the group food, cider, and room in the stables for the night. True to form, Ballar pushed that hospitality beyond its intended limits by simply taking Cobb’s bed for himself.

Not to be outdone in poor judgment, Erelmor attempted to “repay” Cobb’s kindness by slipping him one of the cursed coins. Fortunately, Ieuan caught the exchange and quietly replaced it with two pieces of legitimate gold before any lasting harm could be done.

The following morning, as the party departed, Ballar and Erelmor took some time on the road to discuss the cursed coins in greater detail. They debated what exactly the magic was doing, whether it marked ownership, summoned Eula, tracked the dead, or operated by some more complex rule none of them yet understood.

Their theorizing was interrupted by another grim curiosity. Just off the road lay two dead bodies beside a small burrow. Naturally, this was more than enough to invite immediate investigation.

Enrak leaned forward and peered into the hole. That proved to be a mistake.

Two swarms of stirges burst out and descended upon the party with savage speed, latching onto Enrak and Ballar and driving their proboscises into flesh with alarming efficiency. Blood flowed quickly, and for a moment the whole encounter threatened to become far more dangerous than such miserable vermin had any right to be.

The party responded swiftly. Erelmor struck from range with unnerving precision, while n’Dong, in one of the more baffling tactical choices of the journey so far, cast Vicious Mockery at the swarms while bending down to theatrically suck at the corpses in what appeared to be an attempt to unsettle the stirges through imitation. Whatever the logic, the creatures were eventually destroyed.

With the immediate danger passed, the group examined the bodies. They looked freshly dead, and both wore a horrible rigor-mortis grin stretched across their faces. A second inspection of the burrow turned up a tightly sealed vial and a scroll of Lesser Restoration. The party then pushed the corpses down into the hole, and Erelmor, never one to miss a chance at experimentation, tucked a cursed coin into one dead man’s pocket to see if it would summon Eula. It did not.

They carried on, but less than an hour later something far stranger took hold. Without warning, Ballar, Ieuan, Erelmor, and n’Dong all began to giggle, snicker, and then laugh uncontrollably, each unable to understand what exactly was so funny. Only Enrak was spared.

A quick and alarmingly competent medicine check from n’Dong revealed the truth: the afflicted had contracted Cackle Fever, more commonly called the Shrieks. The disease caused fits of incapacitating laughter under stress and, if left unchecked, could become deadly.

Ballar acted at once, using his paladin’s cleansing powers to purge the disease from himself. The effort left him drained, however, and unable to offer the same cure to anyone else for the remainder of the day. With no better option, the group agreed to continue traveling while keeping distance between the infected and the healthy, hoping to find shelter before the disease or the road killed them. The road had one more cruelty in store before granting them that mercy.

With Ballar and Enrak riding slightly ahead, Ballar spotted danger on both sides of the trail: a cluster of giant centipedes to one side, and a giant scorpion lurking to the other. Recognizing the fragile state of his companions, he made a snap decision. Rather than alert the others and risk provoking one of their laughing fits, he simply continued riding forward as though nothing were wrong, hoping they might all slip through unnoticed. The scorpion had other ideas.

It scuttled out into the path, blocking their way and throwing the group immediately into combat. As battle began, Ieuan and Erelmor were both seized by the Shrieks, wracked with helpless laughter in their saddles and unable to act. At that moment, n’Dong made a choice that would define the encounter. Judging the numbers to be the greater threat, he unleashed his breath weapon across two groups of centipedes.

In an instant, the problem multiplied. What might have been a manageable confrontation with one major predator became a nightmare of nine enemies, all active and enraged.

Enrak paid the price first, swarmed by centipedes and dragged down into unconsciousness while n’Dong himself barely remained upright. The scorpion hammered at Ballar, who looked around and quickly recognized the full scope of the disaster: two allies laughing helplessly, one unconscious, one on the brink, and the enemy pressing in from all sides. His conclusion was simple and brutally practical. Get out.

Ballar healed Enrak just enough to bring him back, then turned Kingcup and fled the field at speed. As he escaped, Ieuan and Erelmor managed to fight through their laughter and regain control. It was not enough to salvage the battle. Enrak, only just restored, was immediately overwhelmed again and dropped. n’Dong and Ieuan, panicked and recognizing collapse when they saw it, also fled.

The scorpion then turned on Erelmor, slamming its claws into him and bringing him down as well. Fortunately, Vesper had more sense than most of her rider’s companions and bolted from the melee, carrying him to safety.

That left only Enrak, unconscious in the center of the chaos, with Bracken standing firm beneath him. Against all reason, he stirred back to consciousness one final time and managed a desperate escape.

The party galloped away for what felt like hours, not daring to slow until the sounds of pursuit had fully faded. By the time they finally stopped, the whole group had been reduced to a miserable, rattled, and thoroughly humbled state. They agreed that after such a hellish day of travel, rest was no longer optional.

Ieuan was tasked with finding a suitable place to make camp, and to his credit he found one: secluded, defensible, and nearly perfect for the long night ahead. After everything the road had thrown at them, it was the first decision all day that had gone entirely right.
