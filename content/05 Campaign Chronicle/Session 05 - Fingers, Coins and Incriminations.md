# Session 05: Fingers, Coins and Incriminations

> [!abstract] Session summary
> The Thistlewound massacre, cursed coins and a roadside ambush deepen the conspiracy around Anthea and Perfid.

## Thrones, Fires and Fingers

Smoke still clung to their lungs and blood to their boots as the party stepped out of the [[The Sundering of Ardynia|Ardynian]] ruin, hoping for daylight, perhaps clarity. Instead, they found **carnage**.

What had once been the **clearing of Thistlewound** - quiet, still, strangely untouched — was now a stage set for horror. Bodies lay in ruin, **twisted and torn**, blood slicking the grass, steel shattered, the stink of death rising thick and wet from the soil.

The party spread out, hearts racing, stomachs tight.

- **Ieuan**, ever perceptive, knelt by a corpse that didn’t belong—a **drow**, out of place, his tunic torn and stained in dark violet. No one knew who he was. But someone had wanted him dead.
- **Enrak** examined another, noting the **brutality of it all**—both **blades and claws** had done the work. This wasn’t clean. It wasn’t professional. It was **desperate**.
- **Ballar** found what remained of **Buggie**, the bugbear warlord who’d once ruled this sorry patch of forest. He tore through the corpse, searching—urgently, almost obsessively—for the **strange silver coin** he'd handed over just hours earlier. He found **nothing**.

Unseen by the others, **Erelmor** slipped away. Perhaps out curiosity or his rogue instinct to act alone. He crept toward the edge of the massacre, eyes peeled, bow nocked. A branch snapped. He spun, arrow ready—but held his fire just in time as **Terrick** emerged from the trees, eyes wide, a single **terrified goblin** trailing behind him like a lost child.

The party reconvened, pressing both survivors for answers, but got little more than fragments. The attackers — whoever they were — had come **looking for something**. A **note**, they said. Stolen by Buggie’s gang.

That was enough for **Ballar**, who wasted no time declaring the goblin their **“guest.”** His tone didn’t leave room for refusal.

**Terrick**, understandably, protested the idea of returning to [[Somnolent]] without rest — but was overruled. There was **something hunting in these woods**, and the party didn’t intend to sleep where it might find them. As **n’Dong, Enrak, and Ieuan** decided to search Buggie’s Den, **Ballar** made a quiet request of **Erelmor**: a trophy. Something to show **Alistair Prusset**. Something to prove Buggie was truly dead. A **finger**, perhaps.

Erelmor, ever the dramatist, had a grander vision—**the head**. Unfortunately, enthusiasm far outpaced skill. He hacked away, **missing the neck entirely**, tearing through **muscle and bone**, coating himself in bugbear blood like a mad butcher. When it was finally too much even for him, he took the finger. Just the finger. A **sickening rip**, and it was done. But something else dropped from the grasp of the dead bugbear chief; an insignia ripped from one of the assassins — one Erelmor immediately recognised as belonging to his old allies **the rootbound**.

Meanwhile the rest of the party inspected the cave that had once echoed with goblin songs and bullywug croaks. There, **n’Dong** uncovered a **hidden compartment** beneath the stone throne. Inside: **50 gold**, and more importantly, **the strange silver coin**. The one Buggie had demanded be returned to him.

Then, for reasons only the gods might understand, **n’Dong, Enrak, and Ieuan** decided to **torch the camp**. Whether it was vengeance, cleansing, or pure spite was unclear—but the smoke rose high into the night as they **stumbled coughing** from the cave, eyes stinging, lungs scorched.

They emerged to see Buggie's corpse defiled, a shocking scene of Erelmor's "handiwork". n’Dong vomited on the grass.

## Blood for gold

With all “loose ends” tied, corpses looted and burned, trophies in hand, and a shell-shocked goblin in tow, the group turned toward the **moonlit path back to Somnolent**. Still bloodied from the ruins, lungs raw from smoke, and nerves stretched thin, the party trudged deeper into the night. Their path home was far from peaceful—and fate had no intention of granting them rest.

They found them by moonlight: a **ragtag band of outlaws**, deep in debate. Sat in a loose circle, they bickered over a **haul of gold**—not how to spend it, but whether to **keep it** at all. Voices cracked with unease. Fear and greed clouded their thoughts, hanging over their fire like smoke. It should have been a warning.

But **Ballar**, bold as brass and tired of walking, stepped into their midst and invoked the image of Talik the laughing coin as he sang: *“Spinner of fortunes.”.* It didn’t go down well. Though the bandits outnumbered them and the party was battered, bruised, and sleepless, **the Squishy Boys did not falter**. As blades were drawn and magic flared, battle erupted in the forest clearing.

As the clash began, their **goblin prisoner** seized his chance. With Terrick fumbling behind, the goblin darted into the trees—gone like a whisper. **Ballar**, still dripping with the blood of the bandit leader he'd just crushed, rounded on Terrick with a **burning fury**, all but roaring his incompetence before launching into the woods in pursuit.

Meanwhile, **n’Dong’s magic missile** carved through the remaining bandits like divine judgment, ending the skirmish in a flash of arcane light. Back at the treeline, **Ieuan**, flanked by a sheepish Terrick and a furious Ballar, somehow managed to persuade the goblin to stay put, despite Ballar making no attempt to hide that the goblin would die in Somnolent.

But something **stranger** was happening behind the battleline. As **Erelmor** plucked a **scimitar** from the fallen bandit leader and immediately tossed it away with an audible sneer (“worthless”), a **hooded woman** appeared from **thin air**—no footfall, no warning, only presence.

Without urgency or fear, she began to **collect the bodies** of the fallen with a unerring professionalism. She looked to Erelmor, calm and businesslike: “Sixty gold, for the effort.”

He snatched the gold like a dragon claiming tribute. “See you again soon,” she said, with a knowing smirk. Then she was **gone**, vanishing into nothing as if the trees had swallowed her whole.

**n’Dong and Enrak stood frozen**, mouths open, the hairs on their arms prickling. It was **not right**. Something **deeply wrong** had just happened. When the group reconvened, **Ieuan listened grimly** as n’Dong and Enrak relayed what they'd seen. Whatever **magic** or **power** had just traded with Erelmor—it wasn’t natural. It wasn’t safe.

They turned their attention to the **bandit’s gold**, hoping to salvage something useful—only to find it **tainted**, laced with **arcane markers**. Tracking magic. Cursed money. Erelmor, still holding his bounty, curled his fingers tighter. They warned him. Pleaded. He *accused n’Dong* of hoarding gold—**the irony lost entirely** on him.

Eventually peer pressure swayed the decision, Erelmor threw 50 gold into the bandits pile. **Ieuan**, ever patient, watched him like a schoolmaster catching a student cheat. He didn’t need wildshape to see the greed. He simply folded his arms and waited.

Eventually, **begrudgingly**, **Erelmor gave up the last of the cursed coin**, flinging it away with the petulance of a child forced to return stolen sweets.

## Fighting against the current

With the cursed coins discarded and the woods behind them still humming with uneasy silence, the party resumed their journey back to Somnolent. The strange hooded woman—who had so casually collected corpses and vanished into mist—remained a dark question on everyone’s mind. There were no answers to be found in the trees, only more shadows. But the path ahead, unfortunately, held familiar dangers.

As they neared the river crossing once more, the haunting sound of rushing water stirred an involuntary shudder in Ieuan, his last encounter with the waterfall still fresh in memory. Ballar, less sentimental and more impatient, bristled at the delay and announced his intention to go first—commanding the goblin prisoner to follow once he was across. With the force and flair of a seasoned athlete, Ballar gripped the rope swing and sailed through the air, landing with practiced ease on the far bank. What he didn’t expect was the welcoming committee.

From the underbrush emerged two robed figures—**priests of Elgira**. Ballar had landed in their trap, one cast *Zone of Truth*, the other *Hold Person*. Ballar resisted the first with sheer force of will, but the paralysis took hold. Locked in place, unable to speak or move, he listened as they coldly demanded to know the whereabouts of the **stolen letter from Prusset’s study**. Ballar held his nerve, managing to obfuscate that the note was in his bag. The priests gave their judgment in the form of cruelty—**kicking Ballar into the river** like a sack of meat. He crashed down the waterfall, armor and limbs colliding with rock and current, vanishing from view.

From the far bank, the party watched the scene unfold and erupted into action. Firebolts, acid and conjured daggers flew across the river in a hailstorm of retaliation. The battle quickly turned savage. **Radiant flames** licked through the trees as the priests retaliated, catching **Erelmor off guard** and dropping him to the ground, unconscious. In the chaos, the goblin prisoner—finally sensing true freedom—made its escape, darting into the undergrowth and out of their grasp for good.

Dragging himself from the river with water clinging to his skin and fury in his veins, **Ballar broke the paralysis through sheer rage**, staggered upright, and locked eyes with the cliff above. With a roar, he **charged up the 30-foot incline**, scaling it with maddened determination and unleashing his gem of brightness in a wave of light that lit the enemy up like a beacon. The rest of the gang followed his lead, their anger boiling over. The last of Elgira’s enforcers fell shortly after—**crushed beneath steel and spell**.

As the dust settled, **Ieuan rushed to Erelmor’s side**, stabilizing him with practiced hands and a rare moment of tenderness. The party, battered and breathless, took a moment to breathe. Across the river, the rope swing still hung like a dare.

With Ballar alone on the far bank, Ieuan took it upon himself to rally the team, offering **Guidance and encouragement** in equal measure as each member made their crossing. The moment of calm was almost pleasant—until **Terrick**, who had contributed little during the battle beyond missed shots and nervous mumbling, **somersaulted elegantly across the ravine** with a flourish. The entire party stared, somewhere between disbelief and disgust. It took all of Ieuan’s inner strength, but he followed next—**conquering his fears** and landing safely at last.

Bruised, soaked, exhausted, and still haunted by too many unanswered questions, the group finally saw the flickering lanterns of **Somnolent** on the horizon. The town had not yet stirred—still cradled in the final hours of night. Wordlessly, they agreed to rest. The sun would rise soon, and with it, **new threats, new truths, and perhaps darker tides still to come**.
